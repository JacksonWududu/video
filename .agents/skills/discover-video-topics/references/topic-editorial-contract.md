# Topic editorial contract

## Channel and audience

- Serve Chinese students and early-career users, primarily ages 20–32.
- Cover social systems, financial and economic literacy, psychology and behavior, philosophy, and practical-life decisions.
- Help the audience understand an important question rather than prescribe an individualized action.
- Keep financial topics educational: no stock recommendations, price predictions, return promises, gambling or recovery methods, loan funnels, or individualized financial advice.

## Episode shape

Mature each topic into one ordinary-language question that does not require prior knowledge of the professional term. The proposed 100–240 second episode must contain:

- one recognizable problem or life tension;
- one central conclusion;
- one primary knowledge point and no more than two genuinely necessary supporting points;
- one useful boundary, counterexample, observation, or reflection question.

Use the recurring original white cat to express conflict through action, expression, props, memory, or internal contrast. Treat white-cat visual feasibility as an editorial constraint, but do not replace a supportable user-supplied topic merely because another topic would be easier to illustrate.

## Topic diagnostic

Use these dimensions as the workcard rubric when scoring, maturing, or validating a topic:

- `central question`: one answerable question with no concept pile-up;
- `audience tension`: a concrete experience or decision the audience can recognize quickly;
- `channel fit`: a clear connection to one of the channel pillars;
- `evidence path`: credible research, official data, or authoritative sources can support the core claim and an important limit;
- `scope`: the question can be answered responsibly in 100–240 seconds;
- `safety`: no prohibited financial, gambling, medical, legal, or other high-risk prescription;
- `visual potential`: the central conflict and explanation can be shown clearly, including with the recurring white cat when appropriate.

Use scoring as an editorial diagnostic, not as an automatic rejection threshold. For a user-supplied topic, block Gate 1 only for a material evidence, scope, safety, or production-feasibility problem. Preserve the user's intent and disclose any required reframe for confirmation.

## Gate 1 user judgment

Let the user select one source option by its original number or submit a different topic directly. A strikethrough title only signals that the option appears in selected-topic history; it remains selectable by the same original number. The user may also identify scenes, expressions, or examples they do not want used.

A safe and supportable directly supplied topic is already the user's selection; do not generate alternatives or ask the user to select it again. Keep Gate 1 open only when a material issue requires resolution or an explicitly confirmed reframe.
